Art Nouveau

Art Nouveau emerged across Europe in the 1890s as a deliberate rejection of the industrial world's straight lines and mass-produced repetition. Its practitioners drew their formal vocabulary from plants, insects, and the human body, and insisted that ornament should grow from the structure of an object rather than being applied to its surface afterward. The style governed everything from posters and book covers to entire buildings and transit entrances, and its core conviction was that there should be no boundary between fine art and the designed object.

Architecture

Victor Horta, Hôtel Tassel, Brussels, 1893

Generally considered the first Art Nouveau building. The iron staircase column rises like a plant stem, and the railings, floor mosaics, and wall murals all repeat the same curving line vocabulary. Where does the structural ironwork end and the decoration begin?

Hector Guimard, Abbesses Métro entrance, Paris, 1900

Guimard designed 141 Paris Métro entrances between 1900 and 1913, all from the same modular system of cast iron components. The iron uprights function as structural supports for the glass canopy, but they curve and branch like organic forms rather than standing straight. The "Métropolitain" lettering is hand-drawn to match.

The Poster

Alphonse Mucha, F. Champenois Imprimeur-Éditeur, 1897

Mucha's posters from the 1890s established many of the conventions that students recognize as Art Nouveau even when they do not know the style by name: a central figure framed by a circular halo, hair and fabric flowing outward until they merge with the decorative border, and a muted palette drawn from natural materials rather than industrial pigments.

In Mucha's work the frame, the figure, and the ornament are all part of one continuous composition. The border is not a container placed around the image; it grows out of the image itself. What is background and what is foreground?

The Palette

Art Nouveau's colors come from its subject matter. These are not chosen for contrast or brand identity but derived from the materials the style depicts: leaves, stone, skin, aged metal.
