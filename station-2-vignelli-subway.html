Vignelli Subway Program

By the late 1960s the New York subway carried decades of accumulated signage that contradicted itself from one station to the next, so the Transit Authority hired Massimo Vignelli and Bob Noorda to design a system that any rider could read at speed. Their 1970 Graphics Standards Manual specified everything: the typeface, the black sign bar, the placement of arrows, and the colored disc that identifies each line. Signs fabricated for the system today are still built from that manual. The signs below are classroom recreations, and every choice on them comes from the same small set of rules. As you read them, consider what color is used for in this language.
